Double Exposure Effect Tutorial

Learn how to create a surreal double exposure effect by combining a portrait with a city using blending modes and clipping masks.

Steps Done: 0/18

Chapters Complete: 0/3

Chapter 1: Subject Isolation and Background

Incomplete
1

Go to Select > Subject to automatically select your portrait, then click the Add Layer Mask button to remove the background. Rename the layer "Subject."

2

Create a Solid Color layer, choose any color, and click OK.

3

Drag the solid color layer to the very bottom of the layer stack, underneath your subject layer.

Chapter 2: Blending and Masking the City

Incomplete
4

Drag and drop your city image onto the canvas, placing it above your subject layer.

5

Change the city image layer's blending mode from Normal to Lighten.

6

Right-click the city image layer and choose Create Clipping Mask to confine the city image to your subject.

7

Double-click the solid color layer's color thumbnail, then click an area of the subject to sample its color. Click OK to help the subject blend smoothly with the background.

8

Select the city image layer and click the Add Layer Mask button.

9

Select the Brush Tool and choose a Soft Round Brush. Set the brush opacity to 50% and flow to 50%.

10

Use the left and right bracket keys ([ and ]) to adjust the brush size.

11

Make sure the city image's layer mask is selected and the foreground color is black. Gently brush over parts of the face to restore facial details and refine the double exposure effect.

Chapter 3: Camera Raw and Final Touches

Incomplete
12

Press Ctrl + Shift + Alt + E on Windows or Cmd + Shift + Option + E on Mac to create a merged copy of all visible layers.

13

Select the merged layer and go to Filter > Camera Raw Filter to begin the final adjustments.

14

Under the Edit panel, adjust the basic settings: Contrast to +50, Shadows to +60, Blacks to -100, Temperature to -5, and Tint to +6.

15

Continue adjusting the colors: set Vibrance to +50 and Saturation to +15.

16

Enhance the image details by setting Texture to +100, Clarity to +40, and Dehaze to +50.

17

Set Vignette to -30 in the Effects panel to darken the edges and pull attention to the subject.

18

Open the Optics panel and set Vignette to -30, as shown in the tutorial. Click OK to apply the adjustments and finalize your double exposure.
